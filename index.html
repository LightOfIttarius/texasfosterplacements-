The safety score

The safety score runs from 0 to 100. It compares each open general residential operation in Texas with the others, using state inspection findings. Each finding is weighted by how serious the state rated it (5 points for the most serious down to 1 point for the least), the weighted total is divided by the number of inspections, and homes are ranked. A higher score means fewer weighted findings per inspection than other Texas homes. The score is relative: it shows how a home compares with others, not whether it is safe in an absolute sense.

The inspection record rating

Homes with a score also get a plain rating of Good, Average, or Poor based on fixed standards. A home with a state corrective or adverse action on record can never be rated Good. The rating is based on state inspection findings only. It does not describe quality of care, staffing, or daily life for children, because that information is not published.

Who gets a rating

Only homes with 5 or more inspections get a score and a rating. Homes with fewer show a preliminary Early signs note instead. It is based on very little information, new homes often receive several findings at their first inspections, and it can change quickly. It is not a score and should not be treated as one.

External findings

Some homes list findings from public sources such as court monitor reports, state actions, and news reporting. Each one shows its category, a summary, its source, and its date. These are separate from the score and rating. They are matched to homes by name or operation number, so a match could be wrong, and a finding may be out of date. Please check the linked source, and tell us about any error so it can be corrected.

What is not in the score

Staff-to-child ratio, staff turnover, discipline and restraint practices, dietary information, education program quality, therapy actually provided, extracurricular activities, community involvement, and building and environment safety are all Not reported. The state does not publish them, so we do not guess. The "Licensed services" and "Licensed to treat" tags show what a home is approved to offer, not how well it does it.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The state's inspection counts cover an unknown time period, so homes that have been open longer may have more findings on record.
  • The state's online compliance history leaves out violations until a home has had due process, so recent problems may be missing.
  • The state says its violation weights are not intended to be used as a ranking or score. This site is an independent analysis and is not endorsed by the State of Texas.
  • Scores and ratings refresh weekly from the state's open data and may shift slightly.
  • No information about individual children is collected or shown.

Corrections

Homes and families who believe any information here is wrong, out of date, or matched to the wrong home can contact the site to request a correction.
