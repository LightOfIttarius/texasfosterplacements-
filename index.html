How to read these scores (tap to open)

The safety score

The safety score runs from 0 to 100. It compares each open general residential operation in Texas with the others, using state inspection findings.

  • Each inspection finding is weighted by how serious the state rated it. The most serious findings count five points, and the least serious count one.
  • The weighted total is divided by the number of inspections the facility has had. This keeps a facility from looking worse just because it is inspected more often.
  • All facilities are then ranked from most to fewest weighted findings per inspection. A higher score means fewer findings per inspection compared with other Texas facilities.

The score is relative. It shows how a facility compares with others, not whether it is safe in an absolute sense.

The Inspection record rating

Facilities with a score also get a plain Inspection record rating of Good, Average, or Poor. Unlike the score, the rating uses fixed standards and does not depend on how other facilities are doing:

  • Good: 1.5 or fewer weighted findings per inspection, and no state corrective or adverse action.
  • Average: between those standards.
  • Poor: more than 3.5 weighted findings per inspection, or a state corrective or adverse action together with more than 2.5.

A facility with a state corrective or adverse action on record can never be rated Good. The rating is based on state inspection findings only. It does not describe quality of care, staffing, or daily life for children, because that information is not published. As more information becomes available, it can be added.

Who gets a rating

Only facilities with 5 or more inspections get a score and a rating. Each card shows how many inspections they are based on.

Early signs for facilities with fewer than 5 inspections

Facilities with fewer than 5 inspections do not get a score or a rating. Instead, the card shows a preliminary Early signs note: whether the findings per inspection so far are fewer than most, about average, or more than most compared with facilities that do have scores. This is based on very little information, new facilities often receive several findings at their first inspections, and it can change quickly. It is not a score and should not be treated as one.

What affected this score

Each card has a "What affected this score" section. It shows how many findings the state recorded at each severity level, the point weight each level carries, the number of inspections, and any state corrective or adverse action. It shows counts only. It does not describe what happened in any individual finding. Those details are in the state's own inspection records on Search Texas Child Care, and each card shows the operation number to search for.

State action on record

A dark "State action on record" label means the state has recorded a corrective action or an adverse action against the facility. These are rare and serious, so they are shown separately and they limit the rating.

What is not in the rating

Many things that matter to children are not published anywhere, so they are listed as Not reported and do not affect the score or rating: staff-to-child ratio, staff turnover, discipline and restraint practices, dietary information, education program quality, therapy actually provided, extracurricular activities, community involvement, and building and environment safety.

The "Licensed services" and "Licensed to treat" tags show what a facility is approved to offer. They do not show how well it does it, and they are not part of the score or rating.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The state's inspection counts cover an unknown time period, so facilities that have been open longer may have more findings on record.
  • The state's online compliance history leaves out violations until a facility has had due process, so recent problems may be missing.
  • The state says its violation weights are not intended to be used as a ranking or score. This dashboard is an independent analysis and is not endorsed by the State of Texas.
  • Scores and ratings are refreshed weekly from the state's open data and may shift slightly.
  • No information about individual children is collected or shown.

Corrections

Facilities that believe their information is wrong or incomplete can contact the dashboard to request a correction.
