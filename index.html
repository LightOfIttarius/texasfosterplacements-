How to read these scores (tap to open)

The safety score

The safety score runs from 0 to 100. It compares each open general residential operation in Texas with the others, using state inspection findings. Each finding is weighted by how serious the state rated it, the weighted total is divided by the number of inspections, and facilities are ranked. A higher score means fewer weighted findings per inspection compared with other Texas facilities. The score is relative: it shows how a facility compares with others, not whether it is safe in an absolute sense.

The Inspection record rating

Facilities with a score also get a plain rating of Good, Average, or Poor based on fixed standards. A facility with a state corrective or adverse action on record can never be rated Good. The rating is based on state inspection findings only. It does not describe quality of care, staffing, or daily life for children, because that information is not published.

The filter buttons

The buttons at the top show how many facilities fall in each group and filter the list when tapped. The numbers change as you search or choose a county.

Who gets a rating

Only facilities with 5 or more inspections get a score and a rating. Facilities with fewer show a preliminary Early signs note instead. It is based on very little information, new facilities often receive several findings at their first inspections, and it can change quickly. It is not a score and should not be treated as one.

What affected this score

Opening a facility shows how many findings the state recorded at each severity level, the point weight each level carries, the number of inspections, and any state corrective or adverse action. These are counts only. The state's own inspection records can be searched by operation number on Search Texas Child Care.

External findings

Some facilities list findings from public sources such as court monitor reports, state actions, and news reporting. Each one shows its category, a summary, its source, and its date. These are separate from the inspection score and rating. They are matched to facilities by name or operation number, so a match could be wrong, and a finding may be out of date. Please check the linked source, and tell us about any error so it can be corrected.

What is not in the score or rating

Many things that matter to children are not published anywhere, so they are listed as Not reported: staff-to-child ratio, staff turnover, discipline and restraint practices, dietary information, education program quality, therapy actually provided, extracurricular activities, community involvement, and building and environment safety. The "Licensed services" and "Licensed to treat" tags show what a facility is approved to offer, not how well it does it.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The state's inspection counts cover an unknown time period, so facilities that have been open longer may have more findings on record.
  • The state's online compliance history leaves out violations until a facility has had due process, so recent problems may be missing.
  • The state says its violation weights are not intended to be used as a ranking or score. This dashboard is an independent analysis and is not endorsed by the State of Texas.
  • Scores and ratings are refreshed weekly from the state's open data and may shift slightly.
  • No information about individual children is collected or shown.

Corrections

Facilities and families who believe any information here is wrong, out of date, or matched to the wrong facility can contact the dashboard to request a correction.
