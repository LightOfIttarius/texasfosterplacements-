How to read these scores (tap to open)

The short version

Every placement shows two separate things: a safety score and a confidence label. They measure different things, so they do not always move together.

The safety score

The safety score runs from 0 to 100. It compares each open general residential operation in Texas with the others, using state inspection findings.

  • Each inspection finding is weighted by how serious the state rated it. The most serious findings count five points, and the least serious count one.
  • The weighted total is divided by the number of inspections the facility has had. This keeps a facility from looking worse just because it is inspected more often.
  • All facilities are then ranked from most to fewest weighted findings per inspection. A higher score means fewer findings per inspection compared with other Texas facilities.

The score is relative. It shows how a facility compares with others, not whether it is safe in an absolute sense. It is also a safety score only, not an overall rating.

What affected this score

Each card has a "What affected this score" section. It shows how many findings the state recorded at each severity level, the point weight each level carries, the number of inspections, and any state corrective or adverse action. It shows counts only. It does not describe what happened in any individual finding. Those details are in the state's own inspection records on Search Texas Child Care, and each card shows the operation number to search for.

The confidence label

The confidence label shows how much inspection history the score rests on. It counts inspections and nothing else.

  • Low confidence: fewer than 5 inspections. The site shows "Not enough inspections yet" instead of a score.
  • Medium confidence: 5 to 14 inspections.
  • High confidence: 15 or more inspections.

Why a score and its confidence label can look mismatched

A facility can have a high score with medium confidence. That means few findings so far, but on a shorter record, so the score could change as more inspections are added.

Another facility can have a lower score with high confidence. That means a long record of inspections, and the score reflects that record well. In general, a score with high confidence is the more dependable one, whichever direction it points.

Red flags

A red "State action on record" label means the state has recorded a corrective action or an adverse action against the facility. These are rare and serious, so they are shown separately instead of being folded into the score.

What is not in the score

Many things families care about are not published anywhere, so they are listed as Not reported and do not affect the score: staff-to-child ratio, staff turnover, discipline and restraint practices, dietary information, education program quality, therapy actually provided, extracurricular activities, community involvement, and building and environment safety.

The "Licensed services" and "Licensed to treat" tags show what a facility is approved to offer. They do not show how well it does it, and they are not part of the score.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The state's inspection counts cover an unknown time period, so facilities that have been open longer may have more findings on record.
  • The state's online compliance history leaves out violations until a facility has had due process, so recent problems may be missing.
  • The state says its violation weights are not intended to be used as a ranking or score. This dashboard is an independent analysis and is not endorsed by the State of Texas.
  • Scores are refreshed weekly from the state's open data and may shift slightly.
  • No information about individual children is collected or shown.

Corrections

Facilities that believe their information is wrong or incomplete can contact the dashboard to request a correction.
